Every telescope gets a first light — the first night you actually point the
thing at the sky after however many months of building it. It is traditionally
underwhelming. Something is out of collimation, the finder is off, and you
spend the evening fixing rather than looking.

This is that. The site exists, the sections are laid out, and nothing is in
them yet.

## What goes here

Six sections, arranged along a scale bar from the atomic lattice out to the
observable edge. Optics at the small end, telescopes at the large end, and
everything in between placed roughly where its subject actually lives. The
gap in the middle is real: there is not much between a workbench and a galaxy
that I have opinions about.

More soon.
